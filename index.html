First, ask LinkedIn for your connections

LinkedIn doesn't let apps read your network directly, so you'll request a copy yourself. It's free, official, and only takes a few clicks. LinkedIn opens in a new tab. Keep this one open and we'll be right here.

  • On a computer
  • About 2 minutes
  • Arrives by email
  1. 1

    Open LinkedIn's "Download your data" page

    Use the button, it goes straight there. Or find it yourself on linkedin.com:

    1. Me
    2. Settings & Privacy
    3. Data privacy
    4. Download your data
    Open LinkedIn download page Opens in a new tab · sign in to LinkedIn if asked
  2. 2

    Pick "Connections" only, then Request archive

    Choose the option to select specific files and tick Connections. Skip the full archive: it can take up to a day to arrive.

  3. 3

    Watch your email for the download link

    LinkedIn usually emails it within minutes (it can take longer). Download the ZIP and drop it here as it is, no need to unzip. The file inside that we read is Connections.csv.

Why this way?

LinkedIn only lets approved partners read connections through its API. Your own export is the official route. You choose what to share, and you can delete it from Anyone I Know at any time.

  • Names, companies and titles only
  • No messages, no password
  • We never contact anyone for you
